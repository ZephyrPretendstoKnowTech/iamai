**Central Unit 3 finding, with the case (reported, not patched).** The reused mapping over-matches for the purpose §3.2 needs. `matchesSignature` answers "does this policy satisfy the goal's floor" (a coverage predicate), not "is this policy the goal's implementation" (an identity). On the pinned baseline that means one goal pairs with many policies and one policy with many goals: `azure-management-mfa` → 16 policies, `guests-mfa` → 9, `mfa-all-users` → 8, `geo-restriction` → 4, `intune-enrollment-reauth` → 4, `user-risk` → 3, and `device-registration-mfa`/`all-users-no-persistence` → 2. Coverage's `baselineMatches[0]` is therefore not reliably the policy a step should render (for `mfa-all-users` the first match is a block policy, not `… - MFA - AllUsers`). §3.2 assumes a single "the baseline's policy for that goal"; on this baseline the reused signatures do not yield it.
  - **Consequence for Unit 4:** rendering a goal's What to do cannot just take `policiesForGoal(...)[0]`. A render-time disambiguation is needed — the tightest-matching implementation, or the policy whose name/shape the goal names — or the owner tightens the signatures in `data/goals.json` (data, editable) so each goal has one implementation match. Editing the signatures ripples into the coverage verdict tests, so it is raised here rather than changed under a translator prompt. `goalMap.test.ts` pins the over-matched set, so any tightening lowers a count and is noticed.
