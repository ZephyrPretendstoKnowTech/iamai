Your points, answered

Each answer rests on a check of the code, the 13 test tenants, Jon's repository, or Microsoft Learn. The full reports are saved with the proposal.

1Hold off on removing Show completed and Show deferred… move All Work to the left side.

Done in V2Both toggles stay until you've seen the finished product. Tabs read All work | Ready | Up Next | On Hold, with All work selected when the page opens.

2I wouldn't change the naming… reuse existing names when possible… it should still sound technical.

Done in V2Your titles share one pattern: Title Case, an imperative verb, a technical object, 3–6 words (Establish, Close, Turn On, Require, Limit). V2 reuses four of them outright and builds the other four from the same parts. See the roadmap.

Is 8 too many? I checked every way to reach 7. Merging any two policy sections gives one of 9–10 rows that mixes “require MFA” with “block”. Merging among sections 1–3 breaks their own rules (Direction changes nothing in Entra; Emergency Access has its own anatomy). 8 is the fewest where no section passes 7 rows on any tenant. And after the first sitting, sections 1 and 2 collapse to one line each, so a working admin sees six open headings.

4Does Jon Hope have something in his baseline for how he suggests handling traveling users?

Checked: retiring it weakens nothingIn his documentation, yes; in his policies, no.

  • His naming guide describes a CA-TravelingUsers group, temporarily excluded from location-block policies. It's an example of a group to create, and no exported policy uses it.
  • His countries policy has no travel placeholder. Its only mapped objects are your exclusions group and the allowed countries. It excludes two other groups that even his own README doesn't name. IAMAI Planner leaves those out of the written policy by the V1 rule you approved, and you can map one to a travellers group of your own in Baseline mappings.
  • The Direction travel answer is read by nothing. I switched it between “allowed” and “never” on all nine test tenants and no step changed. Its option text also promises “a temporary travellers group” that the plan never creates.

What IAMAI Planner does for travel today stays: the staff email asks people to tell IT before travelling, and the countries step's help-desk guidance is to add the country to the allowed list for the trip's dates, never exclude the person. That's stricter than Jon's group, so the baseline isn't weakened.

Sources: Jon's README L524 and L543–551 at commit 90d9b89; pinned.json 1173–1199; interpretation.json 160–168.

With a trusted IP range in Entra, it suggests that network. With none, it suggests “we have one but it isn't in Entra yet”, which keeps Define the Trusted Network on the plan (your 2026-09-20 rule). The row only reads Doesn't apply when you choose “remote” yourself. No change needed.

§3 §5Section 3 and 5 are heavy. Any chance we can shrink it? We can ONLY shrink it if… no circumstance where value is lost.

Keep both as they areEvery candidate loses something, so by your rule they stay.

  • Section 5 is only heavy on paper. On every P1 (Business Premium-shaped) tenant it's 3 rows: registration, device registration, guests. Five of its eight listed rows are P2-only or conditional. On P2 it's 6.
  • Section 3 really is 5–7 rows, but each candidate to shrink it fails:
    • Folding your own passkey into the campaign would let you be “marked” past it by the follow-up list. Today it's a hard prerequisite.
    • Merging dormant with separate admin accounts joins two different jobs, so one waits on the slower.
    • Moving the authentication strength, trusted network or service accounts group next to a policy puts it below other policies that need it.
    • Merging the two registration steps gives one row that's half ready and half held: two thresholds and six checks.
  • The real shrinking comes from what you already approved: the countries merge, the risk merges, and Doesn't apply rows.
3.1Maybe 3.1 can present a list of active accounts… with no sign-in logs in 30 days… If more than 30 minutes, v1.1.

Smart list: v1.1The basic list already exists. It covers enabled people with no successful sign-in in 90 days, and leaves out disabled accounts, confirmed service accounts, mailbox-like accounts, Teams Rooms and shared devices, and emergency accounts. It shows as one sentence of names, so a real grouped table is the upgrade: admins, never signed in, 90+ days oldest first, guests, and new accounts marked “not yet claimed”. That's about 3–4 hours, so v1.1.

Why 90 days, not 30: Microsoft suggests 90–180 days. At 30 days the list balloons: large goes from 731 to 2,081 accounts, 42% of the directory. And the directory's own last-sign-in date is better than 30 days of logs, because it includes background sign-ins by Outlook and Teams.

Bug found, fix in v1.0On a real tenant, accounts that have never signed in are hidden. Graph omits sign-in activity for them, and IAMAI Planner reads that as “couldn't read”. So the step can say “no outstanding dormant accounts” while nine exist. It's a 20–30 minute fix. It also makes MFA Readiness show these accounts as “never signed in” instead of “unknown”, which is more accurate.

Microsoft Learn: signInActivity; howto-manage-inactive-user-accounts. Code: collectors.ts:258, sets.ts:166–168, 245.

4.6Per-User MFA… Could we just straight up delete 4.6, and if not, present it only if necessary?

Show only when neededDon't delete it. It's the only place each account's per-user state is judged, and it carries the safety rule “disable per-user MFA on the day Require MFA for Everyone enforces, not before” plus the app-password warning.

The scan already reads every account's per-user state reliably, and marks a failed read as unknown. So the step can appear only when an account has per-user MFA on, or when the read couldn't finish. Unknown still shows it; we never hide it on a guess. On a clean modern tenant it simply never appears. Build: 30–40 minutes, just over your line. Decision below.

Licence“Your tenant has x licensing, which means y features have been excluded…” so they don't think they are 100% compliant to Jon's baseline.

Mostly exists; the wording is wrongEvery P1 tenant already gets a collapsed Not licensed (n) group at the bottom of the Plan. It lists each baseline control the licences don't cover and the licence it needs (e.g. the risk policies and PIM activation need P2). What's wrong is its note: “Nothing in the plan waits on these”. That reassures, and nothing says a finished plan isn't the whole baseline. The Completed tile reads “36 / 36”.

v1.0 (about 15 minutes): reword the two existing sentences to say it plainly, for example: “The baseline includes these, and this tenant's licences don't cover them, so they aren't in this plan. A finished plan puts the baseline in place as far as those licences reach, not all of it.”

v1.1: a “Not licensed (n)” line under the Completed tile, the why and a Learn link on each row, a count fix (it shows 6 where it should show 7 on P1), and matching lines on How and Connect.

ROIs there a way where we help the users create ALL of the policies in report-only immediately?… Or is the artificial boundary… beneficial?

The engine already does thisSection position gates nothing. Once Emergency Access and Direction are done, every policy that can be written is Ready · Create on the same day: 13 of 14 on the test tenant closest to yours. The 14th is the countries policy, which waits only on its own location, a few minutes' work. Turn-on is what's ordered, by the emergency drill, the MFA campaign and readiness. So the plan is already “create everything now, turn on in order”. It just doesn't say so, and a top-to-bottom All work view would make it look sequential.

v1.0: say it in one sentence in the Plan's how-to. v1.1: a visible “N policies ready to create in report-only” line. A single “create everything” script is 3–5 hours, because re-running a half-finished bundle would create duplicates, so it needs an existence check first.

Keep the two real boundaries. Nothing is created before Emergency Access, because 18 policies need the exclusions group. Nothing is created before Direction, because its answers change who a policy covers, and a later change restarts that policy's report-only window and throws the early data away.

Two caveats from Microsoft:

  • Report-only policies that require a compliant device can repeatedly prompt Mac, iPhone and Android users to pick a certificate. This already happens today with Require a Managed Device on day one. Decision below.
  • Registration (“user action”) policies collect nothing in report-only, so creating them early gains no data, though it does no harm. Entra keeps sign-ins for 30 days, so creating more than a month before turn-on gains nothing either.

Microsoft Learn: concept-conditional-access-report-only; reference-reports-data-retention; managed-policies. Code: foundations.ts, lanes.ts:14–17, schedule.ts:571–584.

What you might be missing

What the checks turned up that the proposal alone wouldn't show you.

BugNever-signed-in accounts are hidden on real tenants. The dormant step can say “no outstanding dormant accounts” while several exist, so the dormant step isn't to be trusted until it's fixed. Fix in v1.0 (decision F).

GapSeven of Jon's 38 policies appear nowhere, on any licence: not as a step, not in Not licensed, not as a review row. On a fully licensed tenant, finishing the plan would still imply the whole baseline. (Decision C.)

Already liveMacs, iPhones and Android phones get certificate prompts from the report-only Require a Managed Device policy, which is created on day one. (Decision D.)

PausedCorrectness fixes paused this morning for the flow work should still land in v1.0:

  • Off policies go to Report-only, never straight On.
  • The High user risk step's portal instructions give a different grant from your pinned baseline.
  • Large tenants hit the 50,000 sign-in ceiling and get no plan at all. This fix is being built now.

Worth a lookThe When column. Nearly every Ready row shows today's date, because every create is on day one. With All work as the default, that column doesn't order anything. It could say “Now” for creates and show dates only for turn-ons. Not in V1 or V2; tell me if you want it scoped.

v1.1Partial P2 seats. A tenant with P2 for half its people gets the risk policies planned for everyone; only Inventory shows the seat count. Hybrid tenants: the dormant step says disable in Entra, but a synced account is disabled in Active Directory.

Correction to V1V1 said Require MFA to Register a Device doesn't wait on the MFA campaign. It does, through its readiness gate. The order doesn't change, because the campaign sits in section 3.
