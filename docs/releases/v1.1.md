# IAMAI Planner v1.1

Released 2026-10-08 at https://getiamai.com/planner/. `main` moved from `fcf59888` (v1.0,
2026-10-02) to `8fcb697a`: 179 commits in six days, built on the `v1.1` branch with CI on every
push, and checked against two real tenants before it shipped: a two-person tenant that already
runs the baseline, and a thirty-policy E5 tenant with its own design.

The short version: v1.0 could read one tenant and write a plan from most of a baseline.
v1.1 handles every tenant you work with, builds all of Jon Hope's Defense in Depth baseline,
tells the truth about passkeys, and reads in the order you do the work.

## 1. Several tenants, one browser

- **The Account menu is a tenant switcher.** Every tenant you have opened on this device is
  listed with how long ago it was scanned; open any of them, sign out of the open one only,
  or forget a tenant without signing in to it. Same-named tenants are told apart. Each tenant
  keeps its own scan, answers and plan.
- **A wrong-tenant sign-in names both tenants by ID.**
- **Sign-in failures are said in plain words**: a redirect registered under Web instead of
  Single-page application, a sign-in that came back to a page that did not start it, a token
  renewal that timed out (the scan pauses for a sign-in instead of failing). Microsoft's own
  text stays under a toggle. A stopped scan says what to do.
- **Connect's Sign in tile is two buttons and a disclosure.** The Global Reader paragraph is
  gone; the permissions and how to remove them stay one click away.

## 2. The scan reads more, and says what it could not

- **Service principals** (Directory.Read.All, no new consent). A policy that names an
  application the tenant does not have gets the one PowerShell line that creates it, instead
  of a step that never moves.
- **Graph beta agent fields**, so the AI-agent policies in the baseline can be built.
- **Connect and Inventory name the policies Graph's v1.0 API does not return** (today, policies
  with a risk-remediation grant), so the policy count you check first is explained.
- **Who an External authentication method targets** is read from the tenant, so the
  external-MFA group is the tenant's own, not a question.
- **Entra ID P2 seats** are counted against the people a risk policy covers.
- **A tenant where nobody is asked for MFA** is told so, on the Plan and in the briefing.

## 3. Jon Hope's Defense in Depth baseline, built in full

Every policy in the pinned baseline is now a step or has a stated reason not to be:

- **Require a Strong Sign-in for Your Admin Accounts Group** (ADM-Users), asked only where the
  scan finds an admin eligible in PIM; otherwise the plan says why it does not apply.
- **Require a Strong Sign-in for One Emergency Account Everywhere** (BreakGlass -
  TrustedLocations), Microsoft's two-account pattern, using the first emergency account you
  save. The emergency exception accepts no extra controls on that policy.
- **Block High-Risk AI Agents** and **Block AI Agents You Have Not Approved**, Jon's two AGENT
  blocks, in Close the Doors Nobody Should Use.
- **Prepare the Lockdown Kit:** Jon's three ZTCA switches created Off, with a runbook, Entra,
  PowerShell and JSON tabs; the emergency exclusions group stays online.
- **Block Sign-ins From Countries Not Allowed:** the turn-on waits on its lockout warning
  (who signs in from where, admins first), which runs only on the list you saved; a country
  can be marked left out on purpose; Jon's NoExclusions block is the step's optional second
  policy, blocking chosen countries outright, travellers included.
- **Azure Virtual Desktop:** an optional question; left empty, only its own step waits.
- **Jon's travellers and device-exception groups** stay out of the policies, and the
  interpretation records why.
- **The Not in this plan footer** gives every pinned policy a specific reason, grouped under the
  licence each needs.

## 4. Your own policies are matched by what they do

- **Policy identity is the name.** A policy of your own that does the baseline's job exactly
  under another name is renamed, not rebuilt (Ready · Rename). One that does the job
  differently is built beside, and the card says so.
- **Retire Replaced Policies** pairs each old policy with its replacement, names the step it
  waits for, and keeps a policy stricter than the baseline's on its own line with a reason you
  record. The tenant's own guest policy and admin policy are handled the same way.
- **IAMAI never creates a policy whose name already exists**, and two policies with the plan's
  name make the step ask which is its own.
- **A policy in Report-only is never turned On while its companion is still to create.**

## 5. Passkeys, told straight

- **1.3 Configure Passkey Authentication targets device-bound, attested passkeys for everyone**,
  with no AAGUID restrictions, and lists before you save the registered synced passkeys that
  would stop signing in.
- **A tenant with more than one passkey profile** sees each profile on its own line with the
  reason it holds, and a note that several profiles apply to the same people.
- **MFA Readiness names each passkey Device-bound or Synced** in the drawer and the CSV, and
  says a synced passkey stops because 1.3 makes passkeys device-bound.
- **No "approved passkey" or allow list anywhere**: the words follow what the step does.

## 6. The plan reads in doing order

- **A step waiting on a Direction answer** shows its whole procedure and no threshold until
  the answer names its people.
- **A policy step's next milestone is the thing it waits on**, never its own title; a
  prerequisite card never repeats the policy card's reason; an unmeasured readiness names the
  step that will measure it.
- **Under Next, the report-only line** lists only what can be acted on now: a week that is
  over and stopped nobody, or a policy that would have stopped sign-ins, with who.
- **An optional answer a live step waits on** counts under Needs your input and is asked once.
- **Where everyone works remotely**, Require a Managed Device and the Lockdown Kit name no
  office.
- **Renamed for plain reading:** Require a Strong Sign-in for Admins (was phishing-resistant),
  Require a Strong Sign-in for Basic Sign-ins (its grant is Modern MFA + TAP).
- **Create the Policies in Report-only** counts policies, gives each created policy its own
  task, marks the ones already created, and names the step that creates the countries
  policies.
- **Every policy step has the same card anatomy**, Require MFA to Register a Device included.
- **Prepare Your Team for MFA** says what the registration campaign it read is missing, gives
  the Windows Hello for Business people their how-to, and gives each email its own copy box.
- Wording fixes in 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 3.1, 3.4, 3.8, 5.1, 5.3, 6.3 and 6.4, and Codex's six
  audit findings from 2026-09-29, all closed.

## 7. Export and the briefing

- **The printed briefing opens with a one-page cover**: what the plan is, the finish, what
  people will notice and how the risk is managed; the journey starts on page two, and the plan
  is printed by day.
- **Policies as JSON**, every policy step in one file, is its own Export card and is named in
  SECURITY.md as an unredacted surface.
- **The finish tip** says its date assumes the suggested answers.
- **The grounding bundle** carries the dates its readme promises.

## 8. Accessibility and polish

- A Skip to main content link is the first Tab stop on every page; the browser tab names the
  page, the tenant and the product.
- Focus lands on what replaced a pressed control after Defer, Put back, Approve answers or
  Done; focused buttons keep a visible ring in both themes; forced-colour mode keeps a system
  colour on the selected tab, toggles, the track and the readiness dots.
- The account pickers count every match, say Already picked, and never offer the emergency
  access accounts.

## 9. The sample tenant

- On passkey profiles, so it shows 1.3's lockout list; the follow-up sample approves its
  device answers, so it shows the plan moving.

## 10. Under the hood

- CI on every push to `v1.1`, GitHub Actions pinned to commit ids, the unit job given 50
  minutes, a preview build channel that leaves the production build byte-identical.
- Three engine defects fixed (a guest half already present was also being created; High-Risk
  Users in Report-only could be turned On early; the proposed exclusions group named twice).
- The policy packages' unreachable name modules left the library.
- The five open Dependabot bumps taken, and the `source-map-js` alert closed.

## Known, for v1.2

- **Readiness holds that wait for 100%** read as futile on a large tenant. v1.2 opens with a
  sweep of every all-or-nothing readiness gate and its wording.
- **A tenant with its own complete design** is told to retire most of it once the baseline's
  policies are On. A way to decline a baseline setting with a reason, the way Retire allows a
  stricter policy to stay, is the next product call.
