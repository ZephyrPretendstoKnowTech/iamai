IAMAI limitations

IAMAI plans from what Microsoft records, and nothing else. Where something outside those records could change what a step does, the step says so and asks you before anything is enforced. What that covers, and how to check each yourself:

  • Devices that send mail without signing in: Direct Send printers, scanners and some applications. Nothing appears in the records, so a legacy-authentication block can stop one the plan never listed. Ask whoever manages printing and scan-to-email; the step asks before that block enforces.
  • Authentication that happens outside Entra: a federated domain, third-party MFA, applications against on-premises AD. Entra records the sign-in but not what the other system enforced. Check the domain list for federation and the other system's own console.
  • Anything on a longer cycle than the collected records: the quarterly invoice run, the yearly renewal job. It exists, it authenticates, and it has left no evidence yet. Where those exist, keep new blocks in report-only across a full business cycle.
  • Credentials saved on equipment nobody has touched in years: routers, NAS boxes, UPS cards, an old copier. They surface the day something blocks them; a walk through the comms room beats any report.
  • What a partner's own tenant enforces for their people. IAMAI sees their sign-ins here, never their controls; the guest steps ask how much to trust them.

State definitions

  • Proven — Completed MFA in the collected sign-in records.
  • Likely works — A current method with recent use of the app, and no MFA challenge in the records yet.
  • Never prompted — Signed in during the collected window, but nothing required MFA of them: enforcement is their first real test.
  • Possibly broken — A method is registered but nothing shows it working. Verify before enforcing.
  • No method — No MFA-capable method registered. Email and security questions do not count.
  • Not active — No successful sign-in for more than 90 days, or none on record.

Method definitions

  • Phishing-resistant — Passkeys and FIDO2 security keys, Windows Hello for Business, or certificates.
  • App notification — Microsoft Authenticator push approval.
  • Text or call — Phone-based methods only: they work, and they are the weakest tier.
  • None — No MFA-capable method registered.
